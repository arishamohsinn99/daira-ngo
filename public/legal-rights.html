Legal Rights

A plain-language summary of what Pakistani law actually protects for women — not legal advice, just a starting point so you know what to ask for.

Marriage & family

  • Registered marriage & divorce procedure — The Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 requires marriages to be registered and sets out the legal steps for divorce and polygamy — a husband must get permission from an Arbitration Council before taking a second wife.
  • Right to Khula — Under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, a woman can ask the court to end her marriage even if her husband refuses to agree.
  • Family Courts — The Family Courts Act, 1964 gives dedicated Family Courts sole authority over divorce, child custody, maintenance, and guardianship cases, meant to be faster and more accessible than regular courts.
  • Conditions in the Nikah Nama — The marriage contract lets a woman add her own conditions, such as restricting her husband's right to remarry, or delegating the right to divorce to herself (talaq-e-tafweez).

Protection from violence & harassment

  • Workplace harassment — The Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010 makes harassment at work a punishable offence and requires every organisation to have a complaints process.
  • Domestic violence — Provincial Domestic Violence Acts (Punjab, Sindh, and others) criminalise violence against women and children at home and let courts issue protection orders.
  • Sexual violence — The Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act, 2021 set up special courts and faster timelines for rape cases, and created a sex offenders' registry.
  • Forced marriage & denial of inheritance — The Prevention of Anti-Women Practices Act, 2011 criminalises forced marriage, marrying a woman off to the Quran to deny her inheritance, and depriving women of their share of property.
  • Honour killings — A 2016 law closed the loophole that let killers walk free if "forgiven" by the victim's family, making a minimum sentence mandatory in honour killing cases.

Property & inheritance

  • Right to inherit — Under the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1962, women are legally entitled to a defined share of inheritance as a daughter, wife, mother, or sister.
  • Right to own & manage property — A woman's property, income, and mahr belong to her alone — her husband or family has no legal claim over it.

Work & public life

  • Equal pay & safe workplaces — Protected under the Constitution of Pakistan and workplace harassment law.
  • Maternity benefits — Provincial Maternity Benefit laws provide paid leave around childbirth for working women.
  • Reserved seats — A fixed quota of seats in the National Assembly and provincial assemblies is reserved for women.

Identity & everyday rights

  • CNIC & voting — Every woman has the right to her own CNIC and to vote independently, without needing a male relative's permission.
  • Choice in marriage — A woman of legal age has the right to consent to — or refuse — a marriage; she cannot be legally forced into one.
